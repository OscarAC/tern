Carrera · 7 October 2026 ::toc{depth=2} :::macros \gdef\R{\mathbb{R}} \gdef\im{\operatorname{im}} \gdef\norm#1{\lVert #1 \rVert} ::: ## Definitions {#defs} :::definition[Kernel and image]{#def-kernel} For a linear map $T: V \to W$ the **kernel** is $\ker T = \{\, v \in V : Tv = 0 \,\}$ and the **image** is $\im T = \{\, Tv : v \in V \,\}$. Both are subspaces.[^subspace] ::: [^subspace]: Closed under addition and scaling: if $Tu = Tv = 0$ then $T(u + \lambda v) = 0$ for every scalar $\lambda$. :::definition[Rank and nullity]{#def-rank} $\operatorname{rank} T = \dim \im T$ and $\operatorname{null} T = \dim \ker T$. ::: Notation is cheap. The sum $\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^*$ adds the optimum $x_i^*$ of every coordinate; evaluating it on the department cluster costs $5 per term, or $10 at peak hours. The $0$th coordinate is the bias, the norm $\norm{x}$ is never $\$$-valued, the interval $`]0,1[` is open, and `$HOME` is a shell variable, not maths. ## Two theorems {#theorems} :::theorem[Rank–nullity]{#thm-rank-nullity} If $V$ is finite-dimensional then $$ \dim V = \dim \ker T + \dim \im T. $$ {#eq-rank-nullity} ::: :::proof Pick a basis $k_1, \dots, k_m$ of $\ker T$ and extend it to a basis $k_1, \dots, k_m, v_1, \dots, v_r$ of $V$. The vectors $Tv_1, \dots, Tv_r$ span $\im T$, and they are independent: if $$ \sum_{j=1}^{r} a_j\, Tv_j = 0 $$ then $\sum_j a_j v_j \in \ker T$, so every $a_j = 0$. Counting gives @eq-rank-nullity. ::: :::lemma[Injectivity test]{#lem-inj} $T$ is injective exactly when $\ker T = \{0\}$. ::: :::theorem[Dimension and bijectivity]{#thm-bij} Let $\dim V = \dim W < \infty$. Then $T$ is injective if and only if it is surjective. ::: :::proof By @lem-inj, the @lem-inj{ref=title}, $T$ is injective iff $\dim \ker T = 0$. By @thm-rank-nullity that holds iff $\dim \im T = \dim V = \dim W$, iff $T$ is surjective. Equation @eq-rank-nullity does all the work; compare @eq-bound, which only bounds the rank: $$ \operatorname{rank} T \le \min(\dim V, \dim W). $$ {#eq-bound} :::/proof :::figure[The kernel (plane) and image (line) of a rank-one map $\R^3 \to \R^3$.]{#fig-kernel} ![A plane through the origin, and a line through it, in 3-space](img/kernel-image.svg){width=60%} ::: As @fig-kernel shows, @thm-rank-nullity says the two pictures add up to three dimensions. :::recall[When is a square matrix invertible?] When its determinant is nonzero; equivalently, by @thm-bij, when its kernel is trivial. :::
