# Leaves

Leaves: the whole-line `::NAME[…]{…}` grammar, the leaf allowlist (which reaches media, void and custom elements), demotion to a paragraph, and void tags with content. `fig` and `spacer` have no schema entry and are not in any allowlist.

## leaf/video

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf elements#allowlists

The leaf allowlist reaches the embedded elements, so media needs no raw HTML; a bare key is a boolean attribute.

````tern
::video{src=media/tide.mp4 controls width=640}
````

````html
<video class="video" data-t="video" src="media/tide.mp4" controls width="640"></video>
````

## leaf/custom-element

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf elements#names

A valid custom-element name becomes that element, its attributes passed through.

````tern
::tide-clock{station=leith units=m}
````

````html
<tide-clock class="tide-clock" data-t="tide-clock" station="leith" units="m"></tide-clock>
````

## leaf/content-and-id

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf elements#output-contract

A name outside the allowlist is a `div`; the bracket content is inline, and the group's id goes on the element.

````tern
::fig[Caption *x*]{#f}
````

````html
<div id="f" class="fig" data-t="fig">Caption <em>x</em></div>
````

## leaf/no-content

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf

Content and group are both optional.

````tern
::spacer
````

````html
<div class="spacer" data-t="spacer"></div>
````

## leaf/spaces-between-parts

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf

The grammar allows spaces between the name, the content and the group.

````tern
::fig [Cap] {#f}
````

````html
<div id="f" class="fig" data-t="fig">Cap</div>
````

## leaf/content-nested-element

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf syntax-inline#inline-element

Leaf content follows the rules of a container's title: brackets nest, `\]` escapes, code and math are atoms. So an inline element inside the content nests its brackets.

````tern
::fig[Press :kbd[Ctrl]]
````

````html
<div class="fig" data-t="fig">Press <kbd class="kbd" data-t="kbd">Ctrl</kbd></div>
````

## leaf/allowlist-p

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf elements#allowlists

`p` is in the leaf allowlist (and not the container one); author classes follow the name class.

````tern
::p[Lead text]{.lead}
````

````html
<p class="p lead" data-t="p">Lead text</p>
````

## leaf/hr-with-class

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf syntax-blocks#thematic-break

A thematic break takes no attributes; `::hr{…}` is the spelling for a styled rule.

````tern
::hr{.thin}
````

````html
<hr class="hr thin" data-t="hr">
````

## leaf/name-case-sensitive

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf elements#allowlists syntax-blocks#character-classes

Names are case-sensitive and the leaf allowlist is lower-case, so `::HR` is not `<hr>`: it falls back to `div` with the name as written as its class, and its content is ordinary content.

````tern
::HR[x]{.thin}
````

````html
<div class="HR thin" data-t="HR">x</div>
````


## leaf/void-img

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf elements#allowlists

A void element from the leaf allowlist has no content and no end tag.

````tern
::img{src=a.png alt="A cat"}
````

````html
<img class="img" data-t="img" src="a.png" alt="A cat">
````

## leaf/audio-with-content

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf elements#allowlists

A non-void media element keeps its inline content as fallback.

````tern
::audio[No audio support.]{src=a.ogg controls}
````

````html
<audio class="audio" data-t="audio" src="a.ogg" controls>No audio support.</audio>
````

## leaf/void-content

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf

Content on a void leaf gets `leaf.void-content` at the leaf and is emitted after the element, in a `<p>` because block context needs a block.

````tern
::hr[x]
````

````html
<hr class="hr" data-t="hr">
<p>x</p>
````

````diagnostics
1:1 warning leaf.void-content
````

## leaf/prose-demoted

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf

A line starting `::NAME` that is not a whole-line leaf is a paragraph, with an info at the `::`.

````tern
::before and ::after are pseudo-elements.
````

````html
<p>::before and ::after are pseudo-elements.</p>
````

````diagnostics
1:1 info leaf.demoted
````

## leaf/trailing-text-demoted

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf

Text after the content breaks the whole-line match, so the line is demoted.

````tern
::fig[Cap] trailing
````

````html
<p>::fig[Cap] trailing</p>
````

````diagnostics
1:1 info leaf.demoted
````

## leaf/unclosed-bracket-demoted

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf

Content cut off before its `]` (as auto-wrap does) demotes the line.

````tern
::fig[Caption
````

````html
<p>::fig[Caption</p>
````

````diagnostics
1:1 info leaf.demoted
````

## leaf/bare-double-colon

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf

`::` alone is a paragraph, with no diagnostic.

````tern
::
````

````html
<p>::</p>
````

````diagnostics
````

## leaf/spaced-name

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf

A space between `::` and the name makes a paragraph with `block.spaced-name`.

````tern
:: toc
````

````html
<p>:: toc</p>
````

````diagnostics
1:1 warning block.spaced-name
````

## leaf/malformed-group

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf syntax-blocks#positions

A trailing group that does not parse makes the line a paragraph, with `attr.malformed` at the first bad column (the `%`) and nothing else: a leaf with a malformed group is not also `leaf.demoted`.

````tern
::spacer{#f %}
````

````html
<p>::spacer{#f %}</p>
````

````diagnostics
1:13 warning attr.malformed
````

## leaf/malformed-group-after-content

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf syntax-blocks#positions

With brackets before it, the group that does not parse still gets `attr.malformed` once, at the first bad column: the line is a paragraph whose `[…]{…}` is also a failed bare span, which is not reported a second time. The same holds for such a line inside a paragraph.

````tern
::video[Tide at *dawn*]{.a!}

Text
::video[cap]{#v!}
````

````html
<p>::video[Tide at <em>dawn</em>]{.a!}</p>
<p>Text
::video[cap]{#v!}</p>
````

````diagnostics
1:27 warning attr.malformed
4:16 warning attr.malformed
````

## leaf/interrupts-paragraph

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf syntax-blocks#line-algorithm

A whole-line leaf interrupts a paragraph.

````tern
Some text
::spacer
````

````html
<p>Some text</p>
<div class="spacer" data-t="spacer"></div>
````

## leaf/demoted-does-not-interrupt

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf syntax-blocks#line-algorithm

A line that is not a whole-line leaf does not interrupt a paragraph: it continues it, and still gets `leaf.demoted`, which a demoted line carries wherever it appears.

````tern
Some text
::before is a pseudo-element.
````

````html
<p>Some text
::before is a pseudo-element.</p>
````

````diagnostics
2:1 info leaf.demoted
````

## leaf/escaped

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf syntax-inline#escapes

`\::` turns a would-be leaf into text.

````tern
\::spacer
````

````html
<p>::spacer</p>
````

## leaf/toc-depth-single-number

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf elements#output-contract

`{depth=N}` on a toc lists the levels from the lower bound of its range (the schema's `2-3`, so 2) to N; `depth` is consumed, not emitted. A level-1 heading stays out either way.

````tern
::toc{depth=2}

::toc{depth=4}

# Title
## A
### B
#### C
````

````html
<nav class="toc" data-t="toc"><ol><li><a href="#a">A</a></li></ol></nav>
<nav class="toc" data-t="toc"><ol><li><a href="#a">A</a><ol><li><a href="#b">B</a><ol><li><a href="#c">C</a></li></ol></li></ol></li></ol></nav>
<h1 id="title">Title</h1>
<h2 id="a">A</h2>
<h3 id="b">B</h3>
<h4 id="c">C</h4>
````

````diagnostics
````

## leaf/toc-entry-copied-text

spec: syntax-blocks#leaf elements#output-contract syntax-blocks#heading

A toc entry is a link holding a copy of the heading's inline content, flattened as a reference's text is: the link keeps only its text, the footnote reference is left out, and the reference becomes its text.

````tern
::toc

## See [the docs](https://x.org)[^n] and @k

[t]{#k}

[^n]: A note.
````

````html
<nav class="toc" data-t="toc"><ol><li><a href="#see-the-docs-and-k">See the docs and k</a></li></ol></nav>
<h2 id="see-the-docs-and-k">See <a href="https://x.org">the docs</a><sup class="t-fnref"><a id="fnref-n" href="#fn-n">1</a></sup> and <a class="t-ref" href="#k">k</a></h2>
<p><span id="k">t</span></p>
<section class="t-footnotes"><ol>
<li id="fn-n"><p>A note. <a class="t-fnback" href="#fnref-n">↩</a></p></li>
</ol></section>
````

## leaf/toc-entry-reference-target-text

spec: elements#output-contract elements#references syntax-blocks#leaf

A reference in a toc entry is its target's plain text, exactly as in ref text: `@map` reads "The map", without the heading's `<em>`, and `@rn{ref=title}` reads "Theorem 1", the target's default text: `ref=` chooses the text of the reference itself, not of a copy of it.

````tern
::toc

## The *map* {#map}

## About @map

## On @rn{ref=title} today

:::theorem[Rank]{#rn}
Body.
:::
````

````html
<nav class="toc" data-t="toc"><ol><li><a href="#map">The <em>map</em></a></li><li><a href="#about-map">About The map</a></li><li><a href="#on-rn-today">On Theorem 1 today</a></li></ol></nav>
<h2 id="map">The <em>map</em></h2>
<h2 id="about-map">About <a class="t-ref" href="#map">The <em>map</em></a></h2>
<h2 id="on-rn-today">On <a class="t-ref" href="#rn">Rank</a> today</h2>
<section id="rn" class="t-block theorem" data-t="theorem"><div class="t-title"><span class="t-label">Theorem 1</span> Rank</div><p>Body.</p>
</section>
````

````diagnostics
````

## leaf/label-counted

spec: elements#title-slots elements#output-contract syntax-blocks#leaf
options: schema=extended

A leaf counted or labelled by its schema has no title element, so its label is its first child, `span.t-label`, followed by a space when content follows; a reference to it uses the schema's `ref`. `bib` is the documented citation style, `{counter: 'bib', ref: '[{n}]'}`, whose label is its number. A void element (`photo`, an `img`) has nowhere to put a label and gets none.

````tern
::bib[Knuth, *TAOCP*]{#knuth}
::bib{#lamport}
::photo[Osprey]{src=osprey.jpg}

See @knuth and @lamport.
````

````html
<div id="knuth" class="bib" data-t="bib"><span class="t-label">1</span> Knuth, <em>TAOCP</em></div>
<div id="lamport" class="bib" data-t="bib"><span class="t-label">2</span></div>
<img class="photo" data-t="photo" src="osprey.jpg">
<p>Osprey</p>
<p>See <a class="t-ref" href="#knuth">[1]</a> and <a class="t-ref" href="#lamport">[2]</a>.</p>
````

````diagnostics
3:1 warning leaf.void-content
````

## leaf/toc-label

spec: elements#title-slots elements#output-contract syntax-blocks#leaf
options: schema=extended

A toc leaf with a label (`contents: {tag: 'nav', transform: 'toc', label: 'Contents'}`) gets it as its first child, before the list, as any labelled leaf does.

````tern
::contents

## A
### B
````

````html
<nav class="contents" data-t="contents"><span class="t-label">Contents</span> <ol><li><a href="#a">A</a><ol><li><a href="#b">B</a></li></ol></li></ol></nav>
<h2 id="a">A</h2>
<h3 id="b">B</h3>
````
